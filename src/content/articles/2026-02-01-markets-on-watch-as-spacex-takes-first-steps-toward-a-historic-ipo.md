As of this month, Elon Musk's SpaceX is taking early steps toward what could become the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history. Reports from Financial Times and Reuters reveal that SpaceX has lined up four Wall Street banks: Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, and Bank of America to advise on a potential IPO.

Although no date has been confirmed, the selection of these four heavyweight banks signals that SpaceX is at least laying the foundation. In markets starved of big-name listings, this is the kind of deal investment banks dream about.

Founded in 2002, SpaceX has fundamentally changed the economics of space travel. It's rocket technology as well as its Starlink satellite network have become key revenue drivers, all while minimalizing on costs. Thus, according to Reuters, SpaceX was valued at approximately $800 billion in private share sales in late 2025, cementing its status as one of the world's most valuable private companies.

For investors, a SpaceX IPO would offer rare exposure to a dominant player operating across aerospace, defense, and satellite communications. When SpaceX does decide to go public, the market will be watching closely.
