"Diamonds are forever", Shirley Bassey famously sang in 1971 for a James Bond film. Indeed, diamonds have long had the prestige of being one of, if not, the most coveted precious stone globally. There is perhaps no other company associated with diamonds and all their grandeur than the 138 year old De Beers company. However, turmoil surrounding the diamond market this decade has led to the sale of arguably the most famous mining company globally.

Several African Countries, particularly Botswanan and Angola have expressed extreme interest in acquiring a significant stake in the De Beers Mining Company, one of the world's largest and most prominent diamond company with operations spanning Botswana, Namibia, Angola, South Africa and Canada. The interest has risen as the De Beers' parent company, Anglo-American (which has an 85 percent stake in De Beers), a British multi-national mining firm, aims to sell the company by the end of this year (2026) amid the current diamond crash globally and the rise in demand for synthetic diamonds. It must be noted that the De beers company was acquired by Anglo American recently; it was only 14 years ago when the company announced that they had acquired a majority stake in De Beers.

On the 8th of February, Reuters reported that Angola was seeking a '20-30 % stake' in De Beers, echoing Forbes' reports four months prior. The Angola government publicly has expressed its desire to acquire De Beers as they are aligned with the "defense of natural diamonds" as reported by Forbes whilst Botswana are equally as eager to claim full ownership of the company that they already own a 15 percent stake in.

The sale of De Beers is one of the latest signals of the changes in the diamond market, against the status quo of using natural diamonds that have led the market to crash in such a dramatic fashion. Initially, the drop in prices of diamonds was blamed on the post-pandemic boom in the spending on luxury items to make up for postponed events such as weddings now starting to fade away. However, the value of diamonds has continued to plummet even with the expected end of the pandemic spending dip. This is likely a result of a mix of factors, such as speculation over the future of the global economy. That being said, the main perpetrator of the drop of diamond prices is clearly the rise of the sales in synthetic diamonds, which in 2025 recorded a 45 percent share of the diamond market.

Clearly, the diamond market has seen a permanent or at a least long-term shift away from the orthodox sale of natural diamonds to synthetic diamonds which take, quite literally, millions of years less to make. The sales of the De Beers company is symbolic of the fall of the natural diamond market and the uncertainty surrounding it
