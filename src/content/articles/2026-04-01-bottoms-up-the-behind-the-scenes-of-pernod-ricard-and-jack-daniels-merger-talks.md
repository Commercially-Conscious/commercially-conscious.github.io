In March 2026, it was reported by Reuters that two alcohol giants; Pernod Ricard and Jack Daniels were in "discussions" of a potential merger that would combine the second largest spirit maker globally with the largest, and arguably most recognizable, whiskey brand globally.

The Brown-Forman company, the official name and parent company of Jack Daniels, is valued at around $11 Billion. The company, which has existed for over 150 years, boasts a wide portfolio of alcoholic brands, including tequila company Casa Herradura as well as their bourbon maker, Woodford Reserve. Though Pernod Ricard has a marginally higher value of around $16 Billion, it similarly has a plethora of over 200 different brands such as Absolut, Jameson and Malibu.

Clearly, a merger would create an almost monopoly of alcoholic beverage brands in an industry that its already extremely competitive and what a Stellenbosch University article asserted to be as "environment that is becoming increasingly crowded with similar product offerings and competing brands". This leads to one considering whether the possible merger of the two brands will in fact be a long-term positive for the alcohol industry or merely aid in the creation of a monopoly.

The merger would additionally have to navigate the possibility of complying with the complex merger laws of not only the United States' federal government and the respective state (in this case, Tennessee) but additionally comply with French law as Pernod is French.

That being said, a merger would be mutually beneficial for the two mega-brands. Pernod will benefit primarily due to the continuing slump in sakes in spirits because of tariffs and CEO exits.
