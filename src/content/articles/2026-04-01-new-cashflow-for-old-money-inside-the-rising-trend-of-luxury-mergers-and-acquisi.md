There is a rising sub-genre of M&A's that has been creating a significant splash in industry; luxury brands eating up or combining with each other.

Perhaps the most notable instance as of now would be the $4.7 Billion acquisition of Kering's beauty division by L'Oréal announced on the final day of March this year. The deal additionally grants Loreal a substantive fifty yearlong exclusive license to solely create and develop some of Kering's most premier luxury products such as Bottega Venetta and Balaenciaga.

Nicolas Hieronimus, Chief Executive Officer of L'Oréal described the achievement as a "significant milestone" in their "strategic partnership with Kerring". Clearly, this deal will be greatly beneficial for the luxury brand, which already boasts major brands such as Vogue, Cera Ve and Vichy.

However, it appears that there is another imminent second luxury merger; on the 7th of April Reuters reported that the founding families of Puig and Estee lauder meeting that day to discuss a potential merger between the two. Although a merger may appear prima facie counter intuitive for Estee Lauder, which has three times the number of sales as Puig. However, further investigations show that Puig would make a very solid contribution through its strong portfolio of historically well performing fragrances such as Jean Paul Gautlier.

A major challenge that may block the acquisition is that both companies are headed by families, which often has led to difficult negotiations in M&A, likely as there will be many stubborn patriarchs or matriarchs leading the negotiations.
