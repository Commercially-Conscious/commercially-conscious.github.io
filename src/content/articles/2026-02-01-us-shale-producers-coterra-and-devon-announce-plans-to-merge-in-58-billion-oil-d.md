Two Shale producers hailing from the United States, Coterra and Devon Energy, have announced their intention for the two respective companies to merge into a single company. Though the Financial Times initially reported that there is no guarantee for the deal to be closed, on Monday the second of February, both companies confirmed that they will indeed. It would not be controversial to assert that the deal is a logical step for both companies; both Coterra and Devon have operations present in the largest United States oil field, the Permian basin as well as the Anadarko basin respectively. Furthermore, the combination of their operations would give reciprocal benefits to both companies' shareholders as it would give them the ability to seriously compete against larger rivals, which will be crucial at a time where low crude oil prices (partially due to the United States actions in Venezuela).

The structure of the merger means that Coterra shareholders will receive 0.70 shares for every share held and that Devon will in fact have a 54 percent majority share in the combined venture. Reuters calculates that this deal has an value of $21.4 billion.

Clearly, it appears, from face value at least, that Devon will have the driver's seat in this combined operation; along with their shareholders having a majority (albeit, a slight majority) as well as the new company keeping the name of devon rather than Coterra. Furthermore, the CEO of Devon, Clay Gaspar, will lead the combined company whilst Coterra's former CEO Tom Jorden will serve as a non-executive chairman of the board.
