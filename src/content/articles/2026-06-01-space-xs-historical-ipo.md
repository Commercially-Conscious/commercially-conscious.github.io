24 years ago, Elon Musk founded Space X, a cutting-edge Space and Artificial intelligence Company. On the twelfth of June 2026, that same company made him the first trillionaire in human history after it presented Wall Street investors with an initial public offering valuation of one hundred and thirty five dollars per share. Moreover, those who advocate for trickle-down economics will be pleased to hear that over four thousand of the company's workers, nearly a quarter, are millionaires. CNN reported that this was the largest initial public offering ever.

However, the monumental success of Space X seems to be a surprise to many, including Musk himself who, as CNBC reports, gave the aerospace company only a ten percent chance of success. This leads to the question of why Space X was able to offer such a high valuation for it's IPO and which parties have invested in the company.

Clearly, Space X's current profits offer no enticement to investors; last year the company incurred an almost 5 billion dollar loss and incurred an astounding forty one billion dollars in loss over its existence. However, according to PBS news, investors are unperturbed and willing, if not eager, to bet on the future of Space X due to it's promise to provide data centers in outers space for artificial intelligence. This shows that the company is being seen as a future cog in the artificial intelligence boom (which many fear may only be a bubble) .
