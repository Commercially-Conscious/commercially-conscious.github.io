French Luxury giant, LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton SE, has surprised many through reports of the largest luxury company, worth approximately $300 billion, was seeking to sell some of its largest and most famous brands.

This news comes out as luxury brands face a continued slump in consumer spending on their brands; Berenberg reported in 2025 that for only the third time in three decades, luxury sales had declined for two consecutive years. Indeed, LVMH has seen decline amidst this industry-wide slump; Reuters reported in April that the organization's sales had continued to dip, partially due to the economic effects of the ongoing US-Iranian war and the resultant blockage of the strait of Hormuz. The decline in sales has consequentially led to a twenty-six percent drop in sales.

Clearly, the current US-Iranian war is to blame for some of its woes- mall shopping in the Middle Eastern Luxury capital of Dubai has fallen by over fifty percent. However, peace talks between the two has not stopped LVMH from seeking to sell some of its top assets.

The most reported asset that LVMH has an interest in letting go of is the Marc Jacobs brand, which it values at over $1 billion. Bloomberg had first reported the company exploring its options with Marc Jacobs in 2024 and it appears that they now have a concrete goal of offloading the fashion brand which it has owned for nearly thirty years. Further, it has been reported that LVMH are willing to sell Rhianna's Fenty brand. LVMH has a fifty percent stake in Fenty beauty and is estimated to have a value of $1-2 billion.

Although neither of the sales have been completed, LVMH's eagerness to offload the two brands signals a potential reactionary change in how it acquires and manages its brands.
