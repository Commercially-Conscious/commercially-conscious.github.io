Many investors were surprised to wake up on the first week of May to hear the story of GameStop, a company now arguably best known for its meme and internet culture fueled investors, preparing a serious bid to buy eBay at an offer of nearly 6 times of the former's valuation.

GameStop, worth a respectable $11 Billion, made an offer to eBay's investors, one of the world's largest online stores, to acquire the stock for $56 billion.

Aside from the fact that GameStop was attempting to acquire a company worth significantly more than itself, concerns over the financing of the deal were expressed. The company is offering a cash and stock offer worth $125, $20 more than the valuation of the stock.

The company's eccentric CEO, Ryan Cohen has claimed that he believes that he could turn eBay into a platform that could rival the likes of Amazon. Although it may appear that he is seeking to bite more than he can chew, he may have reason to believe he could; last year he oversaw the net profits of the company, which specializes in the sale of Video-game related products, rise from $131 million to over $418 million.

However, many remain skeptic of the company's ability to not only finance but to complete the deal altogether. GameStop rose to prominence during the pandemic as many invested into the company despite it apparently lacking value due to a meme craze in purchasing stock where buyer purchased companies which investor had invested in.

Despite all of this, Cohen still maintains that he will approach the share owners of eBay if the board rejects it. The acquisition will be one of the most bizarre of late if it does occur and it will be one which investors will keep an eye on to see its outcome.
