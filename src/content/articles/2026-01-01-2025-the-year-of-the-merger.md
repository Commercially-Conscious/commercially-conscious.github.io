The biggest news of December 2025 was arguably film companies Netflix and Paramount's blockbuster fight for the acquisition of the Warner Bros. Discovery film company. This was the final major acquisition of a year of significant transactions in the merger and acquisitions (M&As) industry, with notable instances including the 56.6 billion dollar acquisition of Electronic Arts by the Saudi Arabian Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners as well as the 88 billion dollar bid for Norfolk Southern by Union Pacific in July. In fact, the impressive 41 percent rise in the value of M&As to over 4 trillion United States Dollars, the second highest in history, behind 2021, has resulted in [**ionanalyitics.com**](http://ionanalyitics.com/) referring to this past year as 'the year of the megadeal'.

This was no surprise for Goldman Sachs, however. In the last month of 2024, they shrewdly published a report asserting that mergers would accelerate in the following year despite the fact that approximately 26 deals in M&A were abandoned due to anti-trust laws in 2024. What is particularly interesting about the trends in 2025's M&A market is the significant rise in private equity investment, which [**invstopedia.com**](http://invstopedia.com/) defines as when companies and/or corporations "acquire and manage private companies or take public companies private, with the goal of ultimately selling them for a profit."

This all leads to the question of whether these trends will continue in 2026 and the rest of the decade.
