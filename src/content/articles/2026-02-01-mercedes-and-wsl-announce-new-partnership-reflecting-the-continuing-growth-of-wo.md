On the seventh of January 2026, Mercedes-Benz Uk announced that they had become the Official Automative Partner for the Barclay's Women's Super League (WSL) and it's second division, the Women's Soccer League 2 (WSL2)'s Presenting Partner for their new playoff format, where the third placed WSL2 team faces the bottom ranked WSL team similarly to the WSL2's male equivalent, the Sky Bet Championship. This reflects the significant growth that women's football has seen in the past decade; in 2025, there were estimations that women's sport would see revenues of up to$ 820 million. Furthermore, the inclusion of women's teams on popular football video games such as Sports Interactive's famous Football Manager Franchise and the emergence of celebrity footballers such as Barcelona Femeni duo Alexia Putellas and Aitana Bonmatí as well as Arsenal forward Beath Mead has seen a significant rise of women's football in the public eye.

However, FIFA still claims that an astonishing 67 percent of all women's football teams globally are still operating at an annual loss and whilst men's team gain revenue largely from broadcasting rights and matchday revenue, women's football is still extremely reliant on sponsorships such as Mercedes-Benz's aforementioned deal with the WSL, with sponsorships consisting of 55 percent of total women's football revenue. Despite this, there is still evidence that the matchday revenue will rise with locals taking increasing attention to women's sport. In 2024, an Arsenal vs Manchester United WSL match saw over sixty thousand fans attend at Arsenal's Emirates Stadium and since England's Lionesses' 2022 Euro's victory, there has been a significant rise in viewership of the WSL. Clearly, there is still a lot of progress needed for women's sports to reach the same popularity and viewership heights as the men's.
