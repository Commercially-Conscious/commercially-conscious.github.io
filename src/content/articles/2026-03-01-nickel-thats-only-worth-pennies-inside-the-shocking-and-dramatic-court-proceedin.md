Perhaps one of the most dramatic cases to come out of mercantile law this past year has been the heated dispute between Indian tycoon Patreek Gupta and multinational commodities giant Trafigura. In a hotly contested court ruling, Gupta was ordered to recompensate Trafigura an excess of $700 million after the Geneva based company accused Gupta of masterminding a Ponzi-scheme like ploy in which he promised to supply 99.8 percent pure nickel through his companies. The reality was starkly different than what was promised; most of the nickel provided by Gupta was either barely valuable or was completely worthless. According to Reuters, Gupta did accept that he failed in his obligation in delivering high-grade nickel cargoes but countered that it was in fact the Trafigura staff devised the scheme, something Trafigura's former head nickel trader Sokratis Oikonomou denied.

Clearly, the judge in the hotly contested judgement did not believe Guptas allegations of the Trafigura staff being involved in the scheme. The UK judge ruled that Trafigura's staff were "wholly innocent of any wrongdoing". Gupta contested this decision, appealing to the United Kingdom's High Court. An intriguing aspect of this court case is that a large portion, possibly 3 million pounds, of Gupta's appeal was funded by not one but by two companies controlled by a member of the UAE's royal family, Sheikh Mohammed Bin Sultan Bin Al Nahyan as Trafigura's barrister, Mr Nathan Pillow says claims. However, a February judgment upheld the previous court's decision and though there is still opportunity for Gupta to appeal to the United Kingdom's Court of appeal, in the time being Gupta is still obligated to follow the follow the worldwide order of the freezing of his assets.
