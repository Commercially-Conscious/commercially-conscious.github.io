April 15, 2026, was a landmark day for recent competition law. A jury seated in the Southern District of New York ruled that Live Nation Entertainment, Inc. and its subsidiary, Ticketmaster LLC (which merged with Live Nation in 2010) had created an illegal monopoly in the entertainment industry.

This was the result of a hotly contested debate of whether or not the companies unlawfully conducted business through tying their ticketing services to their amphitheater and concert services through its business, ticket master, which distributes tickets for a plethora of events, including sports and music.

It has been well documented that a monopoly offers little to the economy and will in fact typically negatively affect the economy through high prices and offering lower prices with fewer options. This has indeed been seen in Ticketmaster and its control over the ticket market; fifty years ago, a ticket would have sold for $10 but now that has climbed to over $130 on average.

This led to the Department of Justice (hereafter the "DOJ"), strengthened by an army of over thirty state attorney generals, filing a lawsuit against Live Nation in May 2024. The DOJ relied on the 1890 Shearman anti-trust Act, which was the first Federal Act to effectively outlaw monopolies in the United States, as the basis for their lawsuit. Although the DOJ settled with Live Nation before the case went to courts, 32 states and the District of Colombia, unsatisfied with what they viewed as a "slap on the wrist" on Live Nation, continued with the lawsuit.

The plaintiffs alleged that a monopoly had been created in contravention of Section 2 of the act, which states that "Every person who shall monopolize, or attempt to monopolize, or combine or conspire with any other person or persons, to monopolize any part of the trade or commerce among the several States, or with foreign nations, shall be deemed guilty of a felony". Furthermore, the plaintiffs argued that Live Nation additionally was non-compliant with Section 1 of the Act, which stated that "Every contract, combination in the form of trust or otherwise, or conspiracy, in restraint of trade or commerce among the several States, or with foreign nations, is hereby declared to be illegal".

Clearly, from the aforementioned statistics provided, the plaintiffs had sufficient grounds to argue that, at least according to the Shearman Act. Indeed, the court agreed and deemed that Ticket Master and Live Nation are an illegal monopoly in as they monopolized both the ticket selling market as well as the amphitheater market through forcing artists and stadiums to use their products for virtually all events, giving them virtual control of the prices and products offered in the concert and events industry.

Though the decision appears prima facie to be a resounding victory for anti-trust advocates, many skeptics and concertgoers alike are now, perhaps reasonably, asking if this decision will be of any impact.

According to Vox, Live Nation is expected to appeal the court's decision, likely on the basis that it already settled with the DOJ. Additionally, there is a pessimistic view on the future on tickets price; streaming leading to artists gaining less money from music than performance will likely keep ticket prices high.
